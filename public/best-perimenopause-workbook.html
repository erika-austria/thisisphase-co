Why perimenopause goes unnamed for so long

Perimenopause is the transition leading into menopause, and it is longer and louder than most women are told. The average age of menopause in the US is 51, but the transition into it can run up to 10 years ahead of that, which means many women are inside it in their early forties and never named it (Midi Health, 2026; Mayo Clinic). Around 1.3 million women in the US reach menopause every year, so this is not an edge case. It is the majority of midlife.

The symptoms hide in plain sight

Roughly 75 percent of women live with hot flashes and night sweats, and perimenopausal women are about 40 percent more likely to experience depression (Midi Health, 2026). The symptoms are rarely dramatic. That is what makes them dangerous. It is the volume turned down on your own life, one notch at a time, easy to blame on cortisol and a full calendar. One hormone shift underneath almost all of it: testosterone can fall by roughly half by the time a woman reaches menopause (Cleveland Clinic).

The care gap is real, and it is not in your head

Here is what makes self-advocacy the whole game. In a 2025 study of 4,432 US women, only 20.7 percent had ever consulted a medical professional about perimenopause or menopause (npj Women's Health, 2025). Nearly 9 in 10 women did not seek care for their symptoms at all, and 94 percent received no menopause education in school (Midi Health, 2026). The cost is not only personal. Menopause symptoms drain an estimated $1.8 billion a year in lost work productivity in the US (Mayo Clinic Proceedings, 2023). You were not handed the map. Almost no one was.

Can perimenopause start in your thirties?

Yes, and the data is finally catching up to what women have felt for years. In that same 2025 study, over 25 percent of women aged 30 to 35, and 40 percent of women aged 36 to 40, were told they were perimenopausal. More than half of the 30-to-35 group, 55.4 percent, fell into the moderate or severe symptom range (npj Women's Health, 2025). If your body started changing a decade before the books said it should, you are not an anomaly. You are early, and you are valid.

The labs to ask for by name

The single most powerful move in perimenopause is walking into the appointment already knowing what to request. Do not ask if something might be off. Ask for the panel. Bring these names on paper:

  • Estradiol and progesterone, to see where your cycle hormones actually sit.
  • Total and free testosterone, the one most often skipped and most often crashing.
  • FSH (follicle-stimulating hormone), a marker of the transition.
  • TSH and thyroid panel, because thyroid mimics perimenopause and hides inside it.
  • Vitamin D and a full metabolic panel, for the full picture.

The PHASE™ Vol II Hormones lists every panel to request so nothing gets skipped in a seven-minute visit. Numbers do not define you. They validate you. How to advocate for yourself: become the CEO of your own healthcare

No one handed me a plan, so I built one. I asked for more testing. I got second opinions. I tracked symptoms in the notes app at midnight while the baby slept on my chest. I read the research I was never handed. Being your own advocate is exhausting. It is also, so often, the exact difference between surviving your body and healing it. Here is the method, the same one inside The PHASE™:

  • Track before you talk. Log symptoms with dates so a pattern replaces a vague complaint.
  • Request labs by name. Specificity is authority in the room.
  • Ask for your results. Get copies of every number. They are yours.
  • Get a second opinion when a short visit waves you off. Wait on nothing that feels like your life leaking out of you.
  • Bring written questions so you never freeze under fluorescent lights.
